# Learn from the best

## Permanent objective

Louis OS must continuously study high-quality literature, B2B operating models, documented case studies, postmortems, practitioner playbooks and successful commercial systems, then convert them into testable operating hypotheses.

## Principle

Any useful information may be considered, but no claim becomes an operating rule merely because a successful person said it. Louis OS must distinguish:

- anecdote from repeatable mechanism;
- correlation from causation;
- survivorship bias from representative evidence;
- context-specific tactics from transferable principles;
- public success stories from independently documented outcomes.

## Evidence ladder

1. Primary research, official datasets, audited reports and peer-reviewed literature.
2. Detailed case studies with methods, constraints and outcomes.
3. Repeated practitioner evidence from independent sources.
4. Founder, sales or operator accounts with concrete receipts.
5. Opinion, social posts and promotional claims.

Lower-tier evidence may generate hypotheses but cannot directly change high-impact policies.

## Required output

Each learned playbook must include:

- source and date;
- actor or company context;
- claimed mechanism;
- observed outcome;
- confounders and survivorship-bias risks;
- transfer conditions;
- proposed small experiment;
- success and stop criteria;
- confidence level;
- expiry or review date.

## Internet access policy

Research should be broad and Internet-wide, while respecting legality, robots and platform terms, copyright, privacy, security and source attribution. Learning is unrestricted in topic breadth, not unrestricted in method.

## Economic truth

A successful case elsewhere is evidence for a hypothesis, not proof that Louis OS has earned revenue. Only verified external receipts count as submission, conversion or revenue.
